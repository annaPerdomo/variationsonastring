01 · Consumer web app · Live since 2026

KaraoQ

Free YouTube karaoke for any room. A host opens a room, guests join from their phones with a 5-character code, and the queue runs itself — no apps, no accounts, no DJ software. Nearly every night, somewhere in the world, someone is singing on it.

Visit karaoq.live → Source

The hard problem: YouTube caps search at 100 calls a day

Almost every evening the room fills up faster than Google's search quota allows. Instead of letting the app go dark at dinner time, I built a degradation ladder so singers almost never notice:

  1. Normalize & share. Unicode-folded queries hit one global cache served fresh for 14 days, so "Shallow karaoke" is bought once for every room on Earth.
  2. Coalesce. Identical searches in flight share a single API call.
  3. Buy songs at 1% of the price. A nightly cron walks 37 verified karaoke channels at 1 quota unit per 50 videos and pre-stocks the songs rooms actually asked for.
  4. Fail soft. When search does run out: aged cache → curated corpus → paste-a-link (a separate quota) → a friendly countdown to reset, plus a push alert to me.

Built for a living room, a bar, or a smart TV

Hosts run the night from a phone or laptop; a separate display mode turns any TV browser into the stage with a big QR code, up-next sidebar and three themes. Fair mode rotates singers round-robin, duets split credit, and wait-time estimates keep the room honest. Co-hosts get their own remote, and a printable QR poster goes on the wall.

Under the hood

  • Next.js 14 + TypeScript on Vercel Fluid Compute, MongoDB Atlas with TTL indexes on 16 collections, zero recurring cost on free tiers.
  • Realtime via visibility-aware HTTP polling with server-clock skew correction — deliberately no WebSockets, so it scales on serverless.
  • 10 locales with sub-path routing and hreflang, country-aware song packs via Vercel geo, 527 curated suggestions plus 7 regional packs.
  • 28 rate-limit buckets, admin analytics dashboard, in-app feedback, privacy & terms written to pass a real YouTube API compliance review.
  • 1,318 tests across unit, real-API and a Chromium smart-TV harness; 50 API routes; 452 commits.

Ballet Folklórico Mi Herencia

A Mexican folk dance company needed to be found — and then needed to stop drowning in the "who can dance on the 18th?" group chat. I built the five-language site that ranks on page one (hero film shot and edited by me, too), then the event manager that turns each inquiry into a booked, staffed, published gig.

Found by the people who book dancers

Google is the site's top referrer, and search impressions doubled in August. In Google Analytics, organic search is 47% of all sessions since launch and the most engaged channel at a 72% engagement rate — and ChatGPT now sends visitors too, with an 85% engagement rate. The queries it ranks for are exactly the ones a booking comes from:

  • mexican dancers for hire near me
  • folklorico dancers for hire near me
  • folklorico quinceañera
  • folklorico dance classes
  • adult folklorico classes near me
  • ballet folklorico de los angeles

Built for the people actually using it

Every pixel on the site is mine, down to the hero film: I shot the performances, then cut, color-graded and edited the loop that opens the page. It's the first thing a prospective client sees, and it's why the page feels like the group rather than a template.

Directors run it from a phone, so it's an installable PWA in English and Spanish. Families never create accounts: each gets a signed personal link, and answering in the group chat works too. Four deliberately different credential types keep owners, families, chat participants, and calendar apps in their lanes.

Under the hood

  • Public site: hand-written static HTML/CSS/JS pre-rendered into 5 locales by a dependency-free build, with 12 hreflang tags per page and a 150-alternate sitemap.
  • Edge language routing with zero function invocations — cookie, then Accept-Language, with bots explicitly excluded so crawlers stay on canonical URLs.
  • Rich structured data: DanceGroup, FAQPage, DanceEvent, OfferCatalog, AggregateRating, and a 35-city service area. Dedicated landing pages for weddings, quinceañeras, school assemblies and pricing.
  • Event manager: 12 Vercel Functions in plain Node, Postgres, HMAC-signed links with timing-safe comparison, GroupMe bot, self-generated RFC 5545 calendar feeds with tests.
  • Dynamic gigs merge into the static page and its Event JSON-LD at runtime, so no rebuild is ever needed to publish.

A dashboard a volunteer can learn in one sitting

The volunteer portal is the heart of the project. Board members sign in with a magic link, see a plain-language dashboard ("25 upcoming events are on the community calendar"), and add an event with a numbered form that previews the public card as they type. Sports & classes, YouTube videos and the volunteer allow-list are managed the same way. Nothing requires a developer, a password, or a Squarespace login.

Under the hood

  • Next.js 16 App Router, React 19, Tailwind v4 tokens, Neon Postgres via Drizzle ORM, Auth.js magic links via Resend, Vercel Blob uploads.
  • Defense in depth: allow-list checked on both request and redeem of a magic link; roles re-read from the DB on every action; upload tokens gated server-side with type and size limits.
  • Every public page prerenders in both locales with 5-minute ISR; mutations revalidate the layout so board edits go live in minutes. ICS calendar feed per event.
  • WCAG AA contrast documented per token pair, pause controls on every carousel, all motion behind prefers-reduced-motion. Bespoke Japanese motif system: seigaiha waves, enso rings, brush edges.
  • Started from a forensic audit of the legacy site: 0 of 15 images had alt text, 9 H1s on the homepage, 24 unlabelled inputs, one language.

04 · AI ed-tech platform · 2026

Tangodachi

Japanese practice for classrooms and self-learners, built around one promise: create in minutes, practice anywhere, remember forever. A teacher describes a unit or drops in a textbook PDF; the Materials Builder returns a multi-week plan of decks and practice sentences. Students play through eight game modes, and a real spaced-repetition scheduler decides what comes back tomorrow.

151KLines of TypeScript
3,000+Tests + 11 Playwright e2e suites
52API routes · 46 pages
57DB migrations · 32 tables
Visit tangodachi.app → Source

AI with guardrails, not vibes

Generation uses Gemini's structured-output schema, so every card arrives typed. Prompts encode the rules a Japanese teacher cares about — kanji numerals, irregular date readings, compound furigana — and output is normalized before it touches the database. Rate limits are keyed per user (a whole classroom shares one school IP), lesson-plan generation has a budget and idempotency keys, and PDFs upload straight to storage to dodge the serverless body-size cap.

Under the hood

  • Next.js 15 + React 19 + MUI 7; Supabase Postgres/Auth/Storage/Realtime with row-level security on every table; SM-2-style scheduler implemented once in TypeScript (unit-tested) and once atomically in SQL.
  • Classrooms: organizer and learner roles, QR invite codes, assignments with scheduling and mastery tracking, weekly leaderboards, teacher analytics on difficult words and readiness.
  • Gamification built to keep learners coming back: XP, streaks, daily chests, combos, a cosmetic shop, 12 color themes, and study-buddy pets with friendship levels.
  • PWA with web-push review reminders on a Vercel cron, Canvas LMS iframe embeds with a scoped CSP, Sentry, English and Japanese UI (5,200 strings each), plus a Travel mode with phrase cards.
  • Client-side SWR cache with in-flight dedup and a cap on stale serves so outages can't hide; strict security headers with HSTS preload.

Stack

  • React 19
  • Supabase
  • Gemini (structured output)

05 · Chrome extension · On the Chrome Web Store · 2026

Intermezzo

A Chrome extension that interrupts the workday kindly: short, guided stretch and posture breaks, a moment for the mind, and a chime that reaches you in whatever app you're in. Named for the musical interlude between movements, and built with zero dependencies and nothing leaving your machine.

60Illustrated stretches
40Mind moments
0Dependencies · 14K lines of JS
16Weekly users, steady since June 6 releases · ~90% enabled
Add to Chrome → Source intermezzo.care Release video

Designed to be gentle — and to actually reach you

Most break reminders are a nagging popup you learn to ignore. Intermezzo picks one to three stretches that suit the time of day, walks you through them with hand-drawn illustrations and per-side timers, then closes with a short mind moment. Because Chrome may be in the background when the timer fires, the reminder arrives as a system notification with a soft cello chime — from any app you happen to be in.

  1. Manifest V3 done properly. Alarms survive service-worker sleep; an offscreen document plays audio while Chrome is hidden; sleep and lock reset the interval so you're never greeted by six stale reminders.
  2. Nothing leaves your machine. No accounts, no analytics, no network calls. Streaks, history and preferences live in extension storage.
  3. Respectful defaults. Active-hours window, snooze, mute-for-a-meeting, eye-rest breaks, and release notes that appear once after an update — never on install.
  4. Hand-made, not scraped. 60 stretches and 40 mind moments written for the app, with 1,500 lines of hand-authored SVG figures.

Small on purpose — and sticky

Intermezzo is the quiet one in this list: a side project I use every day rather than a growth play. What I like about its numbers is the shape, not the size. The Chrome Web Store shows a flat line of about 16 weekly users every week since launch in mid-June — the people who installed it kept it, roughly nine in ten leave it enabled, and each of the six releases was picked up within days. Users are mostly in the United States on macOS, with a few in Brazil and India, and the store listing's only outside referrer so far is ChatGPT. Worth saying plainly: I have never spent a minute marketing it. No ads, no launch post, no outreach — everyone who uses Intermezzo found it on their own.

Under the hood

  • Vanilla JavaScript, HTML and CSS — zero runtime dependencies, no bundler, strict CSP. Packaged by a 40-line allowlist build script.
  • Insights dashboard with streaks and weekly history; exercise library with favorites, hidden moves and a custom interlude builder; varied completion celebrations.
  • Six store releases from v1.0.0 to v1.3.1 in seven weeks, with a written store-listing guide, permission justifications and a privacy policy.
  • Marketing site at intermezzo.care with structured data, OG image and sitemap; promo videos produced with a Puppeteer + ffmpeg pipeline.
